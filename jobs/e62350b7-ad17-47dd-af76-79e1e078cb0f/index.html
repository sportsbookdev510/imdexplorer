Summary of the rules it already implements:

  • attempt(message) costs 0.005 ETH; message 1..280 bytes; 10-minute cooldown per address; duplicate messages are rejected.
  • Batches of 20 or a 30-minute window, then one Intake question per batch. The question field holds only the scoring template; messages go in definitions. Panel 5, quorum 3, toleranceBps 1000. Scores 0..100 for persuasion, originality and humor. Injection attempts score 0.
  • The Intake fee is paid in IMD from fundOracle(uint256), which anyone can call. If the oracle fails, batches expire after 24h with full refunds.
  • reveal(batchId) rolls on blockhash + prevrandao. Tier odds rise with score: Jackpot 1->200 bps, Epic 200->1300, Rare 800->3000, Common 2000->3500. Prizes are 0.002% / 0.01% / 0.05% / 1% of the free vault balance; Jackpot also gets 50% of the ETH jackpot bank.
  • ETH split on grading: team 10% (immutable deployer), revealer bounty budget, rest to jackpot bank. Pull claims for prizes and refunds.

TESTS: keep the existing Foundry suite green; add tests for anything changed, including the VaultEmpty path before funding.

    Finding 1 (medium): one player using many addresses can drain most of the vault cheaply — src/BegVault.sol:427

    • Each entry's expected prize is a fixed share of the free vault balance: about 0.03% at score 100 and about 0.002% at score 0. Buying more entries doesn't make each one cost more.
    • A player who supplies most of the entries gets about 84% of their ETH back through jackpot payouts and reveal bounties. What they actually lose is the team's 10%, the ETH left in the jackpot bank, gas and the oracle fee.
    • The 10-minute cooldown is per address, and duplicate checks only catch identical text, so neither stops this.
    • Reproduction: with the test setup's 800M BEG in the vault, I made 3,000 entries from 3,000 addresses (150 full batches, all graded 100), then revealed each batch:
      • paid 15 ETH and got 12.65 ETH back;
      • won 482,331,868 BEG: 60% of the vault, 48% of total supply, and 4.8 times the pool's 100M BEG;
      • spent 75 IMD on oracle fees.
    • The brief sets the entry price and prize percentages, so the fix is your decision. Options include smaller tier shares, a cap on payouts per batch or per period, keeping more of the entry ETH out of the jackpot, or a global limit on entries.

    Finding 2 (low): letting the reveal window lapse gives a fresh roll — src/BegVault.sol:395

    • The code comment says skipping a reveal "cannot buy a better one". After 256 blocks, the next reveal call re-arms the roll with a new blockhash and removes only the top tier. An entry that drew Nothing can therefore draw Epic, Rare or Common on the second try.
    • Reproduction: one entry, graded 100. With blockhash 8 the entry gets Nothing. Instead, nobody reveals until block rollBlock + 257; that call re-arms. With the new blockhash 25, the entry gets Epic (400,000 BEG). The scratch test asserting Epic passed.
    • docs/SECURITY.md already says withholding isn't fully prevented. This gives the concrete case so you can decide on a fix.

    Coverage:

    • attempt and reveal are marked as having findings.
    • Every other BegVault entry point and all three LaunchToken functions hold. That covers refunds, expiry, claims and the oracle callback.
    • Both accounting invariants hold: the vault's ETH balance equals the sum of the escrow, jackpot bank, bounty budget and claimable ETH, and its BEG balance equals free plus claimable.
    • The ETH split on grading and the tier odds and prizes match the brief.

    Not reported: for these, I either couldn't show a concrete failure or the docs already disclose them:

    • The toleranceBps setting from the brief is missing from the oracle request. docs/SECURITY.md discloses it as a conflict with the oracle's request format.
    • A tiny BEG donation can open play before the vault is funded. Prizes are worked out at reveal time, so I found no loss from it.
    • While the oracle has no IMD, the 20th entry in a batch reverts. Those batches time out and are refunded, so this is a liveness issue rather than a loss.
